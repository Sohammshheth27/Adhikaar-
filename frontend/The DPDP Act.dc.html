The law

The DPDP Act, explained.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules, 2025 give every citizen enforceable rights over their personal data. Here is what the law actually says, cited section by section, and what it means for you.

Your rights

Your rights as a Data Principal.

SECTION 11

Right to access information about personal data

You can ask any organisation that holds your data for a summary of what personal data it has, what it is doing with that data, and the identities of everyone it has shared the data with.

SECTION 12

Right to correction and erasure of personal data

You can require an organisation to correct inaccurate data, complete incomplete data, update it, and erase personal data that is no longer needed for the purpose it was collected for.

SECTION 13

Right of grievance redressal

Every organisation must give you a readily available way to raise a complaint, and must respond to it within the prescribed time before you escalate to the Data Protection Board.

SECTION 14

Right to nominate

You can nominate another person to exercise your rights on your behalf in the event of your death or incapacity.

SECTION 6

Consent

Your consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given through a clear affirmative action. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it.

Their duties

What organisations must do.

SECTION 8

General obligations of Data Fiduciary

Organisations must protect your data with reasonable security safeguards, notify you and the Board in the event of a breach, and erase your data once the purpose is served and retention is no longer required.

SECTION 9

Processing of personal data of children

Before processing a child's data, an organisation must obtain verifiable parental consent, and must not undertake processing likely to cause detrimental effect on a child.

RULE 3, DPDP RULES 2025

Notice given by Data Fiduciary to Data Principal

Every request for your consent must be accompanied by a clear, standalone notice in plain language: an itemised description of the data and the purpose, and a clear explanation of how to withdraw consent, how to raise a grievance, and how to complain to the Data Protection Board.

SECTION 27

Powers and functions of the Board

The Data Protection Board of India inquires into breaches and complaints and can direct remedies and impose penalties on organisations that fail their obligations.

How we got here
Aug 2023

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act is passed.

Nov 2025

The DPDP Rules are notified in the Gazette (G.S.R. 846(E)). The law becomes operational.

Onward

Obligations and enforcement roll out in phases.
